## Who he is

Richard Wiseman's interest in magic began with his grandfather, whose coin tricks fascinated him. When he left school he became a street entertainer, and he went on to study psychology to understand how people react to illusions.[^1] He became one of the youngest members of the Magic Circle.[^2] He earned a psychology degree at University College London and a doctorate at the University of Edinburgh, and became Professor of the Public Understanding of Psychology at the University of Hertfordshire.[^3]

## His contribution to skepticism

Like [Houdini](/skeptics/harry-houdini) and [James Randi](/skeptics/james-randi), Wiseman brings a magician's eye to strange claims: he knows how easily people can be fooled. *Times Higher Education* described him as "on a mission to promote scepticism."[^2]

- **The psychic dog.** A dog named Jaytee seemed to know when his owner was setting off for home. An Austrian TV company tested it, and the result looked remarkably successful.[^4] Wiseman and his colleagues listed the ordinary explanations that could produce the same effect, designed a test that ruled them out as far as possible, and ran four experiments. The data "did not support the hypothesis that Jaytee could psychically detect when his owner was returning home." The study was published in the *British Journal of Psychology* in 1998.[^4] One impressive TV demonstration is an anecdote. A controlled test is evidence.
- **Ghosts and fire-walking.** He has tested for the ghost of Catherine Howard at Hampton Court and debunked fire-walking.[^2]
- **Telepathy.** In 2000, he ran a mass telepathy experiment in London, asking 1,000 people to try to send an image projected onto a building into the minds of volunteers in a sealed room.[^2]
- **Public science.** His research has featured on programs including *Horizon* on the BBC, and his books include *Quirkology*, *59 Seconds* and *Paranormality* (2011).[^3] He has received the CSICOP Public Education in Science Award.[^3]

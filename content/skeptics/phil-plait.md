## Who he is

Phil Plait, known online as the Bad Astronomer, has a PhD in astronomy.[^3] Before joining the James Randi Educational Foundation, he spent ten years doing research with the Hubble Space Telescope, much of it as a contractor at NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center.[^1]

When he was young, he says, he "believed in all sorts of anti-scientific silliness like the Bermuda Triangle, astral projection and the like." He says he owes it all to [James Randi](/skeptics/james-randi).[^1]

## His contribution to skepticism

- **Bad Astronomy.** He started his website in 1993.[^3] On it he analyzes astronomical myths and misconceptions "critically (and humorously)." His first book, *Bad Astronomy*, came out in 2002, and in 2008 *Discover* magazine took on his blog.[^1]
- **The Moon hoax.** In 2001, Plait was sent an advance tape of a Fox special, *Conspiracy Theory: Did We Land on the Moon?*, which cast doubt on the Apollo landings. He wrote an article taking the show apart point by point. Traffic to it exploded, and he heard from people at NASA, including an Apollo astronaut who had walked on the Moon.[^2] It's a good example of the burden of proof in practice: the show made the claims, and each one could be checked against the evidence. Plait says the episode "helped to launch my career as a science communicator and antiscience debunker."[^2]
- **The James Randi Educational Foundation.** He became the foundation's president on August 4, 2008, and served until January 1, 2010.[^4] Randi welcomed him as "a skeptic, a scientist, and a colleague."[^1]
- **Asteroids.** He has also argued for taking real cosmic risks seriously, in his book *Death from the Skies!* and in a TED talk on defending Earth from asteroids. "If we do nothing, then eventually a decent-sized asteroid will hit us," he says.[^3]
